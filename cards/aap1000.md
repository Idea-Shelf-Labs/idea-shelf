# AAP1000

**Claim:** First, a production-ready AP1000. It keeps the design last built at Vogtle, frozen, with only minor approved changes for efficient mass production. Desal, dry-cool, and heat kits come later, attached only where they stay inside the licensed envelopes.

**Status:** ready · lab card · unfinished experiment

**Terms (once):** **AAP1000** = American AP1000-line serial product (U.S. design-certification path; this card's factory chassis). **EAP1000** = European twin of that chassis under EUR / national license — same frozen-NSSS intent, two certificates; not a separately invented reactor class. **NSSS** = nuclear steam supply system (vessel, steam generators, pumps, pressurizer, primary loop). **Standard plant** = certified nuclear island plus the reference turbine island and heat balance the design certification assumed. **BOP** = balance of plant (heat sink, intake, switchyard, desal island, security, site civil). **COL** = combined construction and operating license (US 10 CFR Part 52). **DC** = design certification. **RO** = reverse osmosis (electric desal). **MED** = multi-effect distillation (thermal desal). **ACC** = air-cooled condenser. **EUR** = European Utility Requirements. **FOAK** = first-of-a-kind.

Working published design figures (not measurements from this card): NSSS ~3,415 MWt; heat-balance output ~1,200 MWe gross; net often cited ~1,090–1,117 MWe on the certified wet sink; condenser rejection on that balance on the order of ~2,200 MWt. Kit water output, inland derate, and factory-hour share are **unknown** until a first kit heat balance and a second US unit exist.

## 1. The problem

The AP1000 was sold as modular and factory-heavy. US builds treated it as a unique civil project into an unfinished design. Vogtle 3/4 started with design change still open, including the aircraft-impact shield-building shift to steel-plate composite. Module yards shipped assemblies that failed fit-up or quality on site. Cable-separation walkdowns found hundreds of discrepancies after quality control had signed. V.C. Summer was cancelled mid-build. Unit 4 was faster than Unit 3 on some activities; that is learning inside one two-unit site, not a frozen product arriving at the next COL.

Each new site then reopens what the DC never issued as a catalog: heat sink, water offtake, process or district heat, switchyard, and the civil box modules sit in. That is where custom COL chapters live.

China’s first AP1000 pair and the later CAP1000 series show the family can be repeated under one owner, one regulator, and a localized chain. A 2025 comparison of Chinese series-1 versus series-2 reports large compression of first-nuclear-concrete-to-milestone duration on the second series. That is repetition inside one industrial system. It does not transfer a US yard, a US COL applicant, or US craft. CAP1000 also changed sameness: 50 Hz coolant pumps, Chinese civil standards, localized modules, and — first Chinese series — no US aircraft-impact shield.

A second fully custom European large PWR would split heavy-component shops. Prior “common European PWRs” already did that. Siting and politics stay hard either way.

## 2. The insight

The first product is the repeat plant itself; kits are the second step and must not delay it.

Freeze the NSSS and the standard-plant heat-balance envelope. Put what actually differs by site — heat sink, desal takeoff, optional heat — in pre-analyzed kits at named interfaces. A kit may change net megawatts and BOP civil work. It must not change primary-system design, passive-safety analysis, or the nuclear-island module set.

The bet is narrower than “factory-build the whole plant” and narrower than “wait for an unbuilt small reactor to be the product.” It fails if dry-cool forces a new last-stage turbine, if MED extraction moves steam-generator operating point, or if each COL applicant redraws the island anyway.

## 3. The proposal

### Frozen chassis

Across kits and US sites: vessel, internals, two steam generators, four canned pumps, pressurizer, primary arrangement; passive core-cooling and containment that make the AP1000 safety case; nuclear-island module set and the shield building the current US DC actually covers (one aircraft-impact design, not two); 17×17 fuel inside the certified enrichment/burnup envelope; NSSS operating bands used in the safety analysis (primary P/T, steam-generator pressure and flow, feedwater/steam-flow envelope).

The turbine island is standard only inside a stated envelope: backpressure band, extraction-steam band, condensate-temperature band. A kit that leaves those bands is a new standard plant, not a kit.

### Kits and interfaces

Kits own the heat sink (wet tower / once-through / ACC), the desal island (intake, pretreatment, RO and/or MED, brine, product tanks), and optional heat offtake. Four attachment points only:

1. **Heat sink** — turbine exhaust and condensate return. Backpressure must stay inside the band the standard last-stage blades and exhaust hood were designed for. A Southern Company study on the standardized AP1000 plant stated that then-current ACC practice was not compatible with that turbine without redesign. Default until a later heat balance shows otherwise: dry-cool may not be attachable. Derate **unknown**.
2. **Steam extraction** — declared secondary bleeds (the certified cycle already takes seven feedwater-heater stages). MED or district heat may take additional low-pressure steam only up to a pre-analyzed flow and temperature, through an intermediate heat exchanger. Seawater and district water never see secondary steam. Extraction above the cap changes heat balance and can move steam-generator operating point — the path back into the NSSS case.
3. **Electrical takeoff** — non-safety boards or a dedicated feeder for RO and ACC fans. Net megawatts to the grid fall. Class 1E design does not change. AP1000 safety-related power is DC-battery for the passive case; main AC is not Class 1E. Desal and ACC belong on that non-safety side.
4. **Desal thermal takeoff** — none (RO-only); or LP extraction to MED via the intermediate loop; or condenser-water waste heat. Option three is large in rejected heat (~2,200 MWt class on the published balance) but low grade. Conventional MED wants top-brine temperature around 60–70 °C; PWR circulating-water outlet is often cooler. Waste-heat-only MED is therefore not free. Unknown whether it works on this cycle without extraction.

Must not change when a kit is chosen: primary geometry and materials, passive-safety analyses, nuclear-island module drawings, fuel form and core envelope, Class 1E electrical design, containment and shield building. BOP drawings and site COL chapters may change. NSSS drawings may not.

### Factory versus site

Factory / module-yard: vessel, steam generators, pumps, pressurizer, nuclear-island structural modules, piping and equipment modules, built to a fit-up spec that is not recut on the basemat. Fuel fabrication is already a factory product and is not this bottleneck.

Site-unique, and it stays site-unique: excavation and basemat to local geotech and seismic; circulating-water tunnels or ACC field; seawater intake and brine outfall; switchyard; security barrier; tower or ACC foundations; heavy-haul path. Those scopes were large at Vogtle. They will be large at a coastal desal site. Calling the whole project factory-built ignores them.

What can transfer into US work packages: completed design (no mid-build shield surprise), module dimensional control, cable-separation workmanship, sequencing that does not bury later commodities, shops that have already cut AP1000 heavy parts.

What does not transfer by default: Chinese state coordination of site prep and transmission; 50 Hz machines; Chinese civil-code modules; a labor-cost structure that made field rework cheap; a regulator who licenses one localized plant and repeats it.

### Coastal kit

RO is the clean attachment: electric power, seawater in, product and brine out, no steam-cycle touch. Its critical path is intake (impingement, entrainment, coastal-zone), local pretreatment, and brine mixing — permits that can outlast an NSSS freeze.

MED earns its keep only if low-grade heat beats the electrical derate and capex of more RO, or if the buyer needs MED-purity distillate. Coupling is secondary-side only, intermediate loop, no primary fluid. Hybrid studies usually put RO on bulk flow and MED on a smaller stream, sometimes on RO brine to cut intake volume. That adds a second process island, a second chemistry program, and extraction-valve / heat-balance failure modes.

Working rule, not a measurement: **MED is in the first-kit catalog only if extraction stays inside a pre-analyzed cap that does not move steam-generator operating point.** If that cap cannot be shown on the certified heat balance, the first coastal kit is RO-only. Hybrid is a later kit.

Do not invent m³/day. Size water from the host contract, then check intake, brine, and electrical or extraction load against the frozen envelope. If contracted water forces extraction above the cap, the site is not a kit site.

### Inland dry-cool kit

Turbine exhaust condenses on air. On a hot day, condensing pressure rises, last-stage blades see higher backpressure, electrical output falls. That drop is the derate, and it peaks when a summer-peaking grid most wants the unit.

The buyer has to purchase firm capacity that is explicitly derated on design-hot days, or the plant is not the summer-peak resource. Selling nameplate wet-cooled megawatts off a dry kit is a mismatched product.

If the certified turbine still cannot live on an ACC without a new last stage, the inland offering is a new standard plant. Palo Verde is not this kit: it is a desert PWR on wet towers using treated municipal wastewater.

### Optional heat kit

Additional extraction only as a capped increment on existing bleeds, intermediate loop, unit able to dump the same heat to the main condenser when the customer is offline. If losing the customer forces a different primary or steam-generator mode, the kit has entered the safety case. Omit unless an offtake contract exists before COL submittal. Pipe distance, year-round load, and backup during outage belong in that contract, not in the chassis.

### European twin (EAP1000)

Sharable without lying: heavy-forging pedigree (vessel, steam-generator channel heads and tubing, pressurizer) if the same geometry is dual-stamped ASME III and the European code; fuel form and core envelope if limits are accepted twice; I&C *philosophy* (passive plant, limited safety-related AC, four DC divisions), not necessarily the same qualified digital platform; module dimensional spec from one yard.

Must diverge: EUR and national extras (aircraft-crash spectrum, severe-accident hardware, filtered vent if required), 50 Hz machines, civil codes, the license document. Two certificates.

Failure pattern already on the table: “European PWR” programs that became a Finnish plant, a French plant, and a British plant; CAP1000 versus US AP1000 (50 Hz, civil standards, shield building). The twin is wrong if EUR review redraws the vessel, the shield building, or the passive-safety layout. IEEE versus IEC I&C qualification is how “common I&C” dies; admit platform divergence early.

### Loading aim, not a siting claim

“One or two units per state” is a factory-loading metaphor. A yard and a forging line need a multi-decade order book; spread across the country that *looks like* a unit in many states if sites and buyers exist. It is not an inventory of licensable sites. Several states have bans or moratoria; several have no gigawatt-class interconnection; several have no wet sink and would depend on the dry kit that may not attach. Keep two ledgers: orders per year into one yard, and a screened site list allowed to be short. This card has neither number and will not invent them.

### Ownership

The thesis dies if each utility is design authority and treats the DC as a starting point. One party has to own the frozen NSSS and standard-plant set, the kit catalog and interface calculations, the module yard (or a long-term offtake measured on fit-up, not tons shipped), and supplier qualification for vessel, generators, pumps, and fuel. That can be a vendor-and-yard venture or a multi-utility consortium contractually forbidden to redraw the island. It cannot be “the vendor markets the plant and each applicant hires a different architect-engineer to adapt it.”

## 4. Why this

US design certification and operating US and Chinese units already exist. The choice is to industrialize a licensed large PWR rather than invent a new class and then industrialize that.

The certified plant assumed a wet sink and no water offtake. Without a coastal kit, water-short coasts either refuse the unit or add a custom desal plant the COL treats as a surprise. Without a dry kit that actually fits the turbine envelope, inland sites without a river or wastewater stream are out. Heat stays optional so the chassis does not depend on a customer. The twin exists to keep one heavy-component line across two certificates, not to skip EUR or NRC.

Make-or-break is the interface list. If kit choice amends the NSSS analysis or the nuclear-island module set, the catalog is branding.

## 5. How you'd know

**Freeze:** drawing delta on NSSS and nuclear-island modules from unit *n* to *n*+1; COL for *n*+1 is reference-plant plus site chapters, not a new design-amendment stack; primary operating bands unchanged.

**Factory versus site:** yard hours and rework versus hours after the module is set; share of nuclear-island commodities installed as received versus recut on site. Both unknown for a future US unit; Vogtle is not that unit.

**Kits:** written backpressure band, extraction cap, electrical-board list, desal isolation diagram; COL text that selects a kit by reference rather than rewriting Chapter 10; coastal product water delivered under contract (measured m³); inland net output on a design-hot day versus the wet reference; heat offtake contract plus a demonstrated condenser-reject path when the customer is offline.

**Twin:** dual-stamped heavy forgings, same geometry; two complete certificates; I&C platform reported as same, dual-qualified, or diverged — divergence is not renamed “common.”

**Ownership:** one named design authority; a yard contract that pays for accepted fit-up.

## 6. First test

One standard NSSS plus one coastal desal kit. One site with a plausible interconnection and a water buyer. No inland ACC, no district-heat customer, no European certificate, no fifty-state map.

**Precursor, not the test:** paper interface package — heat balance with RO load, extraction cap if MED is included, isolation-loop P&ID, intake/brine concept, list of NSSS drawings that do not change.

**Scored in public:**

1. **Freeze.** COL incorporates the certified NSSS and nuclear-island set with no NSSS drawing change to attach desal. A DC amendment that touches primary systems fails even if the plant is later built.
2. **Attachment.** Desal island contracted against the interface package. If MED is included, extraction stays inside the cap for the whole construction period. Miss the cap → drop to RO-only or fail.
3. **Factory behavior.** Nuclear-island modules built to a dimensional spec with a recorded recut/reweld rate. No numeric pass line here; the baseline is unknown. Fail if recut to match as-built site conditions is the plan, or if the yard ships incomplete modules for field completion as the plan. That is the US FOAK pattern returning.
4. **Water.** Intake and brine permits issued; product delivered to the contracted buyer in the first operating year. If the host required the water and the water is not delivered, the kit failed even if the reactor operates.
5. **Electrical isolation.** Desal loads stay off Class 1E buses.

Later kits remain untested. If this pair cannot stay frozen, the catalog is paper.

## 7. Open questions

- ACC heat balance that keeps existing last-stage blades inside backpressure on a design-hot day — and that day’s net output versus the wet reference?
- Condenser-waste-heat MED on this circulating-water temperature, or does MED always need LP extraction?
- Extraction cap (flow and temperature) that keeps steam-generator operating point inside the certified NSSS analysis?
- Which CAP1000 module and sequencing practices survive US work packages and NRC inspection language?
- Who can refuse a COL applicant’s requested island change?
- Dual-stamp forgings: which shops, and do EUR vessel extras change the drawing?
- At the coastal first site, is intake/brine a longer pole than NSSS freeze?
- What order cadence keeps one yard alive without treating every state as a customer?

## 8. Why it might not work

- Extraction or backpressure leaves the analyzed band. Chapter 10 reopens, then steam-generator operating point, then the NSSS case.
- The standard turbine cannot live on an ACC without a new last stage. Inland sites stay excluded or split the line.
- Intake and brine permits outlast design discipline and force unique tunnels, outfalls, and mitigation. The desal “kit” is a unique civil plant beside a repeated island.
- Modules leave the yard incomplete; site craft finishes them; recut becomes the plan.
- No owner of the freeze. Each applicant’s architect-engineer adapts the island; the vendor cannot refuse without losing the order.
- Hybrid is specified; the extraction cap kills MED; RO-only was the honest first kit; the extra island remains as maintenance load.
- The inland buyer wanted nameplate megawatts on the hottest afternoon. The contract dies, or the operator overruns backpressure.
- Heat customer never signs, or signs and vanishes. Extraction hardware remains as analysis burden.
- Twin diverges at the vessel or the shield building. Two products, two shops.
- Forgings, generators, pumps, or qualified welders do not match even a modest loading cadence.
- Another licensed design takes the scarce customers, craft, and forging slots.

Wrong in one line: the first unit advertised as a repeat still needs a new primary-system analysis, a custom cooling design that changes the turbine, and a unique nuclear-island package — or a desal attachment that cannot run without redesigning the island.

## 9. Stress-test

**“Factory-built.”** Earthwork, basemat, intake, outfall, ACC or tower field, switchyard, and security are site work. Modularization moved a real fraction of the nuclear island off site and, on the US FOAK, dragged a real fraction back through rework. Honest claim: factory the NSSS and nuclear-island modules to a fit-up spec. If yard-accepted modules still show systematic field recut on the first test unit, drop “factory-built.”

**“About one or two units per state.”** Loading cartoon that reads as a siting plan. Many states host zero units under law, transmission, seismicity, water, or consent. It invites a state-by-state fight that does not test the chassis. Replace it with two numbers this card does not have and will not invent: target units per year into one yard, and a screened site list allowed to be short. Until then: order book sufficient to keep one yard and one heavy-component line alive.

**“Kits do not reopen the license basis.”** Heat-sink changes the turbine boundary condition. Extraction changes the heat balance the steam generators sit on. Those are the doors into the NSSS case. The standardized-plant ACC work already opened the first. MED extraction opens the second unless the cap is shown. A catalog that has not published both numbers is untested. §6 closes only the desal door, and only if the team will drop MED.

**“The twin shares the product.”** Shared name plus shared fuel-form language is how previous common-PWR programs talked while drawings diverged. Countable evidence: dual-stamped vessel / steam-generator geometry and a dimensionally identical nuclear-island module set. I&C platforms and civil codes will diverge; write that down now.

**“The first test proves the line.”** One coastal unit proves one kit interface and one yard cycle. It does not prove dry-cool, heat, the twin, or loading. Passing §6 and selling the full catalog as demonstrated is how this becomes a poster.

**Ownership.** If the design authority is the COL applicant, freeze is optional. If it is a vendor that cannot refuse custom work, freeze is optional. The missing behavior: a buyer who wants an island change is told to wait for a line-wide amendment or to walk. No US large-PWR program has shown that.

The card is wrong, as opposed to slow, if a completed first-test COL amends NSSS documents to attach desal; if an inland offering quietly includes a new turbine as part of the “kit”; or if the twin’s vessel drawing is not the US vessel drawing.
